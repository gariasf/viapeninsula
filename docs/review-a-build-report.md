# Review a build report

Each daily build, and each deploy whose build changed, diffs its report against the last one published and writes the diff to the run's job summary (#250): the spots new since the last build, those gone, and those whose numbers moved. Each spot comes with its key, its lines of the log, and links to the map there, which rings it until the next tap (but not a run of Stations left out on a branch, whose view is fitted to show it), to OpenStreetMap there, to edit OpenStreetMap there, and to the ways its Stations are on. On most days it's one line: nothing changed. Why it's built this way is in `docs/research/build-report.md`.

Where a problem spot is new (a run of legs that keeps the feed's shape, a turn-back, a branch, a hop traced more than twice its straight line and 10 km more, a trace that jumps across a Station's tracks, Trips left out, a TRAM Trip whose stop numbers don't run 1…n, a route name of Renfe's long-distance timetable no Network takes, a Network with no Trips on a day, one built from its copy, or a region whose build failed), the build also comments on the standing [Build report](https://github.com/gariasf/viapeninsula/issues/290) issue, which notifies the maintainer: the new problem spots, with their links, and the run's link (#255). It never comments for a length, a node or the measures, which move with any change to the map, nor for a Network's Trips or Closures, nor for buses where their Line has no track, which no fix upstream can draw (ADR-0012), so those are only in the summary.

## A new spot

1. Open its map link and its OpenStreetMap link.
2. If OpenStreetMap is wrong there (a gap in the rails, a wrong tag, a line on an old alignment), fix it in OpenStreetMap, with a changeset comment naming the spot, as "Join the branch to Platja i Grau de Gandia, for viapeninsula #219". Tell the local mappers if it's more than a gap. If it shows a new kind of trap, cut it into a test first (below).
3. If OpenStreetMap is right and the build's rule is wrong, file an issue here with the spot's key and links.
4. If both are right, as at L'Aldea, where R16's Trains do turn back (#63), do nothing: the spot stays in `report.json` and won't show again.
5. If the operator's timetable is wrong, as where Renfe puts a Station on the neighbouring line's track so that its Trips are left out as "El Entrego is 0.4 km off its track" (or, since they're traced through their own Stations, "is off the network"), or the legs to it keep the feed's shape, find the Station where its Trains stop in OpenStreetMap, and add it to its Network's `stations` in `src/networks.ts` (#367): its ID, those coordinates, and the URL of the OpenStreetMap node as its source. The dry run's log lists each one it applies, and the spot shows as gone in its summary. A Station two Lines call at on different tracks, as Los Rosales, can't have one position on both: check each Line's trace first (`kept` and `length` spots) in the dry run. Then tell the operator, with the Station's ID and the coordinates.
6. A Trip that calls at a Station off its feed's shape, or whose times don't fit it, is traced through its own Stations before it's left out (#280), as C2's CIVIS Trips and the Trips to Santander C3's branch Stations are: its trace is a shape its Line has, which the build logs, `<Line>:<n>: <from> → <to>, <km> km long`, and its `trip` spot is gone (a trace that turns back on the way is reported as a shape's is, and one with a hop much longer than its straight line as a `detour`, below). One that's left out all the same has a spot that names why its own trace fails: a Station `off the network`, two Stations with `no path along the rails`, or `it would run … along its track`, and the build logs its trace too, which its Line doesn't have. What to fix is then OpenStreetMap's rails there, as in 2 and 3, or the operator's times. A `branch` spot, the Stations a feed's shape leaves out as they lie off it on a branch, is reported only where a Station of it is called at by no Trip of its Line placed on a trace of its own on the three days built (#601): where each is, the build logs it and has no spot, as nothing on the map is missing, though Renfe's shape lacks them. A Trip of one day serves it only while that day is one of the three, so its spot is gone while it is, and back after.
7. If the operator files under one of a Network's Lines a train that runs on into another Network, as Renfe files FEVE's Bilbao–León train under Cercanías Bilbao's C4, so that the Line's trace runs on to the other (its `length` spot's line says the shape is hundreds of km long where the feed has the track for tens, as C4's did, 332.7 km against 33.4 km, and the log may say two regions share a Stretch), name in the Network's `beyond` in `src/networks.ts` (#368) a Station on the way beyond where the Line's own Trips end that every Trip that runs on calls at, on every day the timetable has: Arla Berrón, the first the train calls at past La Calzada, for C4's. The build then leaves out each Trip that calls there, with the Stations and track only they have, logs it, and names it in the Network's `trips` spot, whose `leftOut` counts them. The Train is no longer one the Network lists, so Renfe's long-distance timetable's twin of it is left to another Network only if that one lists it too (#258).

A fix in OpenStreetMap reaches the map when the build next downloads Geofabrik's extracts, which it keeps for a week. The spot then shows as gone: that's the check that the fix worked.

## A Network with fewer Trips

A Network shows as changed when it has more than a quarter fewer Trips today than the last report has for the same day of the week (#252). The build keeps them all. A holiday runs a Sunday's timetable, so on one there's nothing to do. Otherwise, look in the same summary for Trips left out, as a gap in the rails leaves them, and for the Network's `leftOut`, which counts those its config leaves out (#368), then at the operator's timetable, which may have lost them.

## A Network built from its copy

A Network one of whose timetables can't be downloaded or read, or gives it no Lines, is built from the copy of them that last built it, which the build keeps in `.cache` (#286). Its `copy <network>` spot gives the day the copy was kept and why, and stays until a new timetable gives the Network Lines again, when it shows as gone. A server that's down for a morning needs nothing. If it lasts, open the timetable's URL: a Network with no Lines, as Rodalies in Renfe's file of 5 Oct 2026, needs a new source; a moved file, a new URL in `src/networks.ts`.

## A region whose build failed

The bundle is built by region (ADR-0014), and a region whose build throws goes without new files: the manifest names those of its last build, from the day before today on, and its `region <id>` spot gives why, in one line, with the whole error in the run's log. Its Networks' other spots are carried over from the last report, so they don't show as gone. The other regions are built and published as ever, so the map is whole, but for the failed region's Trains and Lines running out of days: its last build named at most three from today on, so a failure that lasts three days takes the region off the map. A region with no copy of a timetable, as once the Actions cache is lost, fails so; so does a trace that strays more than 5% from its feed's shape, a solver that finds no order (`src/build/order.ts`), or a track file that wouldn't read back as the track built (`trackFile()`, ADR-0015: a bug in `src/pieces.ts`, whose test to add is the track it failed on). Read the run's log for the stack, fix what it names, and run the build again: the spot shows as gone once the region builds. The one build that first publishes a manifest by region, and a build that couldn't read the last manifest, have no files of a last build to keep for a region that fails: the build fails as a whole instead, with the region's name in the error, and nothing publishes or deploys.

## A TRAM Trip whose stop numbers don't run 1…n

TRAM's trip updates name a stop by its number in its Trip, GTFS's `stop_sequence`, and the Trip's call at that number less one is its Station (#344). That holds only where a Trip's numbers run 1…n without a gap, as every one of the 5,576 Trips in TRAM's feeds of 9 and 10 October 2026 did, so the build checks each Trip of the timetables that say `stopNumbers` in `src/networks.ts`, TRAM's two. A `sequence tram:<line>` spot says a Trip of that Line, on one of the three days built, has numbers that don't: its lines give the Trip's ID, how many calls it has and the first one numbered otherwise than its place, and its `trips` number is the most such Trips on any one day. The Trip is built and drawn as ever, but a Train held by its numbers would stand at another stop than its tram's. If TRAM's feed has lost a number, tell TRAM, with the Trip's ID. If it numbers every Trip from another number, as from 0, what reads the number is to change, not the Trips. The spot shows as gone once every Trip of the Line runs 1…n again.

## A route name no Network takes

Renfe's long-distance timetable names each route for its product, and AVE y Larga Distancia and Media Distancia y Avant take its routes by those names, their `routes.names` in `src/networks.ts`: its 660 routes of 10 October 2026 are under 13, and each is taken. A route under a name neither takes, as one of a new product, or of one written otherwise than before, as in capitals ("Intercity" is the one name in mixed case), has its Trips in neither Network, and nothing else in the build says so (#401). A `route <name>` spot names it as written and says how many routes have it. It has no place on the map, so no links. Look at its routes in the file (`routes.txt` of `.cache/timetable-ave-larga-distancia.zip`) and at where their Trips run, and add the name to the `routes.names` of the Network it belongs to, with the rails its Line runs on in that Network's `lines.gauges`, which a test asks for, and in `lines.names` the name the public knows it by: the old one's, where Renfe has only written that otherwise, so that both are one Line. A route no Network should draw wants nothing: the spot stays in `report.json` and won't show again. It shows as gone once a Network takes the name or the file has no route under it.

## A hop traced much longer than its straight line

A Trip that doesn't fit its feed's shape is traced through its own Stations, along the rails from each to the next (6). Where a hop comes out more than twice its straight line and 10 km more (`DETOUR` and its 10 km, in `traceRun()` of `src/build/track.ts`), the build logs it, as `<run>: <from> → <to> is traced <km> km, <n> times its <km> km straight line` (the run's ID is `<Line>:<n>`, as in 6), and reports a `detour <line> <station> <station>` spot, its key giving the hop's two Stations in order of ID, so that the same hop on another run of the Line is the one spot. Nothing fails, and the Trips on that trace are drawn along it where their times fit it; one that would run too fast along it is left out, with a `trip` spot of its own (6). Open the hop's map link, which fits its two Stations in view and rings the point halfway between them, and look at the rails between them. A Station of the hop that isn't where its Trains stop is moved in its Network's `stations` (#367, as in 5); rails with a gap that sends the path round by another line are mended in OpenStreetMap (as in 2); the spot then shows as gone. A hop that is that long on rails that are right wants nothing: the spot stays in `report.json` and won't show again (4).

## A jump across a Station's tracks

Renfe's long-distance timetable has no shapes, so the build traces each run of a Line's Trips through its Stations (#259), with no feed's shape to hold the trace against. An `across <line> <station>` spot is a trace that leaves a Station on another of its tracks than it came in on, as OpenStreetMap has no switch there that leads on from that one, so its track jumps across the Station, by the spot's `metres`, the most any of the Line's runs jumps there: on 11 Oct 2026, Alvia's 32 m at Alcázar de San Juan, where its run the other way jumps 24 m, and AVE Int's 50 m at Figueres-Vilafant. Its Trains are drawn on it all the same (#262). Look at the Station's tracks and switches in OpenStreetMap, and map the switch that's missing (2 above). The spot shows as gone once the build's rails have the fix.

## A spot as a test

`track.test.ts` draws its rails by hand, which suits a rule. A real trap is better kept as it was: OpenStreetMap's rails, the feed's shapes and the Stations around the spot. Cut one only for a spot that shows a new kind of trap, not for every spot, and before fixing OpenStreetMap, so that the test keeps the broken data.

1. `npm run daily -- --dry-run`, for the rails, timetables and `out/report.json` the snippet is cut from.
2. `npm run snippet -- "<spot key>" <name> [km]`. It writes `src/build/fixtures/osm/<name>.json`, with the day OpenStreetMap's data was downloaded and the credits. It holds every way that comes within that many km of the spot (2 unless given), whole, and the spot's Line's shapes there, with the Stations there that their Trips serve. Give a `<lat,lon>` in place of the key to cut round another point, with every Line's shapes there.
3. In `track.test.ts`, trace it along `ownRails()`, as the build does, and check the lines the spot logged, or, after a fix in our code, that it no longer logs them.

A trace needs the Stations either side of the spot: R16 turns back at L'Aldea on its way from Camp-redó, 5 km off, to Ulldecona, 22 km off. A cut too small to hold them keeps the feed's shape instead, or fails on a shape's length. So cut as little as still traces the spot as the build did, round a point between those Stations if that needs less than round the spot, and aim for a few tens of KB. L'Aldea's, 14 km round a point between Tortosa and Ulldecona, is 72 KB.
