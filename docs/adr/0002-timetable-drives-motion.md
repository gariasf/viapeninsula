# The timetable drives motion; live data only corrects it

No live feed is smooth enough to draw as-is: Renfe Cercanías pins stopped and arriving trains to station coordinates (36% of consecutive positions implied more than 180 km/h), FGC's data is 2–4 minutes old and refreshes every ~2 minutes, and the Metro publishes predictions only. So every Train moves along its Trip's track from the timetable, and live data only shifts it in time: its Delay comes from its position while it's Live and moving, and otherwise from the operator, or for Rodalies, the Cercanías núcleos and long distance from its last GPS, as Renfe's figure is often minutes off (#33, #260). Small disagreements are eased in (a Train slows or speeds up but never moves backwards); big ones snap. Live data also says how much of its Trip a Train runs: none where its operator says it won't run (Cancelled), and only part where it says the Train won't stop at some of its Stations, as Renfe says of one it cuts short or starts late. The timetable still drives it along that part (#346).

A Train its report has standing at a Station is held there: its Delay is never so small or so large that the timetable has it anywhere else when it was reported, where that Station is one its Trip calls at (#39). FGC's trip updates lag Geotren by a minute or two, and so had Trains leave Stations Geotren still had them standing at. Renfe's pinned Trains are the exception, and carry on from their last GPS Delay instead: Renfe pins Trains coming into a Station too, and its pinned Stations are stale, so holding them made their jumps worse (#8: 115 against 85). Nor are TRAM's held: the Station TRAM names is the one a Train is at or has just left, not one it stands at, and held there, a Train would stand 40–80 s and then jump a whole stretch, so it runs as late or early as TRAM says, which has most such Trains on their way (#42). A Train standing at its Trip's first Station can be there long before it leaves, off the map, so there it's only held from leaving: its Delay is never so small that the timetable has it gone when it was reported. The Metro's are drawn there all the same while their Block waits at the end of its Line, since the Block has just come in on the Trip before: a Live Metro Train short of its Trip's first Station, as TMB's time has it for as long as it waits, stands at that Station unless the Trip its Block ran in on is still on the map (#105). That's where it's drawn, not its Delay. Where live data knows no such Trip, as on a map just opened, it stands there at once, even while it's still running in: TMB's time can't tell the two apart. And it stays there until a report has it gone, by its Delay, when it was reported: while a Block waits, TMB's ETA for it keeps moving later, so a Train drawn leaving when an ETA said was drawn out on its track, where it can't run back, until the Block really left (#107). So it leaves a report or so late, a median half a minute over #31's replay, and catches up at up to line speed however far behind it's drawn, jumping only where live data has it over a kilometre on, or its Trip over. Held past when live data has it leave, or catching up, its times run as late as it's drawn, so it's leaving now in the follow panel, on its Station's board and nearby. Not one its timetable took out of that Station before live data first placed it there: that would run it back. Once out, it stays out: TMB's ETA for a Block that has left can move later again, as while it waited, and have it back at that Station, or behind where the Train is drawn on its first stretch. It then stands where it's drawn until its Delay catches up, a stall, rather than jumping back (#125). So that a Metro Train is seldom drawn out before its Block is known, a Scheduled one on a Line whose live data names Blocks, while that live data is available, waits at its first Station for its Block, for up to 3 minutes past when its timetable has it leave: drawn leaving on time, it snapped back as its Block was first listed for it, a minute or two late. Its Block's first report takes over there, and with none by then, it leaves as late as it waited and catches up with its timetable at up to line speed. Run that late to its end, one whose Block's reports had stopped was drawn 3 minutes back once the snapshots that placed it had gone. While it waits or catches up, its times run as late as it's drawn, as while it's held. L9's, L10's and the funicular's Trains keep to their timetable, as TMB publishes no predictions for them, and so does every Metro Train while the Metro's live data is unavailable.

Long distance's Trains run as their Line's speed profile has them over their Network's, so that a late MD never catches up faster than an MD runs, nor an AVE faster than 300 km/h: AVE y Larga Distancia runs at 250 km/h at 0.5 m/s², its AVE, Avlo and AVE Int at 300, and Media Distancia y Avant at Rodalies' 160 km/h at 1 m/s², its Avant and Avant Exp at 250 and 0.5 (#260). Renfe's long-distance positions are GPS whatever their `currentStatus` says: only 6 of 177 `STOPPED_AT` were within 500 m of their Station, so none holds a Train at a Station. They carry no time of their own, so the feed's header stands for it, and they freeze: 44% of consecutive polls repeat one, and 177 holds of a minute or more ended in jumps of over a kilometre, up to 73 km on 4 October 2026. So a Train carries on from its last GPS Delay as Rodalies' do, a position unchanged since its report before counting as none, and Renfe's own figure is only what there's no GPS Delay to carry. A kilometre at 300 km/h is 12 s, less than the 20 s a Train takes to ease back, so a Train there is snapped where it's a minute off, as the time rule has it, and not a kilometre (`snap`). Over 12 minutes of 10 October 2026's long-distance data, 113 Trips traced crudely along OpenStreetMap's rails, that was 0.08 jumps for each Train-minute instead of 0.27, and 3.7% of its Live Train-seconds spent standing between Stations for 30 s or more, waiting for live data to catch up with where it was drawn, instead of 1.0%; for the 23 Trains whose position held and then moved, which the tests replay, 0.09 instead of 0.18. Of their 25 holds of a minute or more that ended in a move of a kilometre or more, none had a Train standing where its position was held, one snapped it back 1.8 km, the 72 s it lost while the position was held, two jumped on, by up to 21 km, and four, drawn ahead of where the new position had them, stood 21 to 48 s until live data caught up, where snapped a kilometre off one would have stood and two snapped back.

## Considered Options

- **Plot live positions directly**, as markers that jump from report to report. Rejected: trains would teleport between stations, freeze for minutes at a time, and Networks without positions would show no Trains at all.
- **Snapping a long-distance Train where it's a kilometre off, as Rodalies' is, or 20 s off at its top speed, about Rodalies' kilometre in time (22.5 s at 160 km/h).** Rejected (#260, the maintainer's pick, 10 October 2026): over the same 12 minutes they made 0.27 and 0.18 jumps for each Train-minute against 0.08, for 1.0% and 1.3% of its Live Train-seconds standing between Stations against 3.7%. A minute is what the time rule already calls big, and the only one of the three under #33's 0.1 jumps for each Train-minute for Rodalies.
