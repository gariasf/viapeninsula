# The timetable drives motion; live data only corrects it

No live feed is smooth enough to draw as-is: Renfe Cercanías pins stopped and arriving trains to station coordinates (36% of consecutive positions implied more than 180 km/h), FGC's data is 2–4 minutes old and refreshes every ~2 minutes, and the Metro publishes predictions only. So every Train moves along its Trip's track from the timetable, and live data only shifts it in time: its Delay comes from its position while it's Live and moving, and otherwise from the operator, or for Rodalies, the Cercanías núcleos and long distance from its last GPS, as Renfe's figure is often minutes off (#33, #260). Small disagreements are eased in (a Train slows or speeds up but never moves backwards); big ones snap. Live data also says how much of its Trip a Train runs: none where its operator says it won't run (Cancelled), and only part where it says the Train won't stop at some of its Stations, as Renfe says of one it cuts short or starts late. The timetable still drives it along that part (#346).

A Train its report has standing at a Station is held there: its Delay is never so small or so large that the timetable has it anywhere else when it was reported, where that Station is one its Trip calls at (#39). FGC's trip updates lag Geotren by a minute or two, and so had Trains leave Stations Geotren still had them standing at. Renfe's pinned Trains are the exception, and carry on from their last GPS Delay instead: Renfe pins Trains coming into a Station too, and its pinned Stations are stale, so holding them made their jumps worse (#8: 115 against 85). Nor are TRAM's held: the Station TRAM names is the one a Train is at or has just left, not one it stands at, and held there, a Train would stand 40–80 s and then jump a whole stretch, so it runs as late or early as TRAM says, which has most such Trains on their way (#42). A Train standing at its Trip's first Station can be there long before it leaves, off the map, so there it's only held from leaving: its Delay is never so small that the timetable has it gone when it was reported. The Metro's are drawn there all the same while their Block waits at the end of its Line, since the Block has just come in on the Trip before: a Live Metro Train short of its Trip's first Station, as TMB's time has it for as long as it waits, stands at that Station unless the Trip its Block ran in on is still on the map (#105). That's where it's drawn, not its Delay. Where live data knows no such Trip, as on a map just opened, it stands there at once, even while it's still running in: TMB's time can't tell the two apart. And it stays there until a report has it gone, by its Delay, when it was reported: while a Block waits, TMB's ETA for it keeps moving later, so a Train drawn leaving when an ETA said was drawn out on its track, where it can't run back, until the Block really left (#107). So it leaves a report or so late, a median half a minute over #31's replay, and catches up at up to line speed however far behind it's drawn, jumping only where live data has it over a kilometre on, or its Trip over. Held past when live data has it leave, or catching up, its times run as late as it's drawn, so it's leaving now in the follow panel, on its Station's board and nearby. Not one its timetable took out of that Station before live data first placed it there: that would run it back. Once out, it stays out: TMB's ETA for a Block that has left can move later again, as while it waited, and have it back at that Station, or behind where the Train is drawn on its first stretch. It then stands where it's drawn until its Delay catches up, a stall, rather than jumping back (#125). So that a Metro Train is seldom drawn out before its Block is known, a Scheduled one on a Line whose live data names Blocks, while that live data is available, waits at its first Station for its Block, for up to 3 minutes past when its timetable has it leave: drawn leaving on time, it snapped back as its Block was first listed for it, a minute or two late. Its Block's first report takes over there, and with none by then, it leaves as late as it waited and catches up with its timetable at up to line speed. Run that late to its end, one whose Block's reports had stopped was drawn 3 minutes back once the snapshots that placed it had gone. While it waits or catches up, its times run as late as it's drawn, as while it's held. L9's, L10's and the funicular's Trains keep to their timetable, as TMB publishes no predictions for them, and so does every Metro Train while the Metro's live data is unavailable.

Long distance's Trains run as their Line's speed profile has them over their Network's, so that a late MD never catches up faster than an MD runs, nor an AVE faster than 300 km/h: AVE y Larga Distancia runs at 250 km/h at 0.5 m/s², its AVE, Avlo and AVE Int at 300, and Media Distancia y Avant at Rodalies' 160 km/h at 1 m/s², its Avant and Avant Exp at 250 and 0.5 (#260). Renfe's long-distance positions are GPS whatever their `currentStatus` says: only 6 of 177 `STOPPED_AT` were within 500 m of their Station, so none holds a Train at a Station. They carry no time of their own, so the feed's header stands for it, and they freeze: 44% of consecutive polls repeat one, and 177 holds of a minute or more ended in jumps of over a kilometre, up to 73 km on 4 October 2026. So a Train carries on from its last GPS Delay as Rodalies' do, a position unchanged since its report before counting as none, and Renfe's own figure is only what there's no GPS Delay to carry. A kilometre at 300 km/h is 12 s, less than the 20 s a Train takes to ease back, so a Train there is snapped where it's a minute off, as the time rule has it, and not a kilometre (`snap`). Over 12 minutes of 10 October 2026's long-distance data, 113 Trips traced crudely along OpenStreetMap's rails, that was 0.08 jumps for each Train-minute instead of 0.25, and 3.2% of its Live Train-seconds spent standing between Stations for 30 s or more, waiting for live data to catch up with where it was drawn, instead of 1.0%; for the 23 Trains whose position held and then moved, which the tests replay, 0.09 instead of 0.18. Of their 25 holds of a minute or more that ended in a move of a kilometre or more, none had a Train standing where its position was held, one snapped it back 1.8 km, the 72 s it lost while the position was held, two jumped on, by up to 21 km, and four, drawn ahead of where the new position had them, stood 21 to 48 s until live data caught up, where snapped a kilometre off one would have stood and two snapped back.

## Considered Options

- **Plot live positions directly**, as markers that jump from report to report. Rejected: trains would teleport between stations, freeze for minutes at a time, and Networks without positions would show no Trains at all.
- **Snapping a long-distance Train where it's a kilometre off, as Rodalies' is, or 20 s off at its top speed, about Rodalies' kilometre in time (22.5 s at 160 km/h).** Rejected (#260, the maintainer's pick, 10 October 2026): over the same 12 minutes they made 0.25 and 0.16 jumps for each Train-minute against 0.08, for 1.0% and 1.3% of its Live Train-seconds standing between Stations against 3.2%. A minute is what the time rule already calls big, and the only one of the three under #33's 0.1 jumps for each Train-minute for Rodalies.
- **Renfe's own figure, instead of a GPS Delay no report before it supported, where none has counted in the last half hour (`delayBy()`).** Rejected (#410, 11 October 2026). Over #329's five recordings (6, 7 and 10 October), 65 of the 359 Delays that a Call took from such a GPS Delay were 10 minutes or more early. They were 9 reports, one each of 9 Trains, each among its Train's first GPS fixes in the recording, so that none had counted: 6 carry exactly another Trip's position from the same snapshot, 3 (2 of the 6 among them) are 3.8 to 34 km off their Trip's track, one is on its track with no other report there, and one is a Train whose own figure was 34 minutes early. Going by the figure for them left 5 of 463 Delays 10 minutes or more early, all one Train's, but drew more Trains ahead of their GPS and then standing: Renfe's figure moves in whole minutes (#33) and is under its GPS far more often than over it (#329), and 22 of Rodalies' 23 new stalls of 30 s or more, and 116 of Madrid's 123, were a Train standing 40 s or so while the figure it went by stepped up a minute. Rodalies' stalls of 30 s or more went from 46 to 67 and Cercanías Madrid's from 605 to 717, Madrid's jumps from 2,078 to 2,198, and the Live Train-seconds drawn 30 s or more ahead of live data from 25,376 to 33,014 across Renfe's Networks, with Rodalies' 0.075 jumps for each Train-minute unchanged; on 10 October's other two recordings, Madrid's jumps went from 933 to 1,076. Taking a fix no report of the Train within two minutes could have run to as no position, which wasn't built, took 6 of the 9 out on the same recordings, and Madrid's jumps down by 2% and its stalls by 5%.
- **A minimum dwell at TRAM's calls (20 s, 30 s): a call's departure no earlier than its arrival plus the dwell, so long as the quickest run to the next stop still fits, for TRAM's Trips only.** Rejected (#405, 10 October 2026). Over #344's replay of 7 October's two weekday recordings, stalls of 30 s or more (a Live Train standing still, more than 20 m from every call, for 30 s or more) went from 64 to 85 to 135 at 14:03 and from 88 to 121 to 162 at 17:47, and the Train-seconds drawn more than 5 s ahead of their reports from 18,658 to 20,425 to 22,329 and from 24,547 to 26,730 to 28,937. Jumps forward and back went from 14 and 11 to 17 and 13 to 24 and 19 at 14:03, and from 16 and 15 to 16 and 18 to 22 and 20 at 17:47. Moving a call's arrival earlier by up to the dwell, and keeping its departure, did no better: 74 and 78 stalls at 14:03, 95 and 96 at 17:47. Why, as far as read and not measured: `delayOf()` takes a tram's Delay from where its report puts it along its Trip, against when the Trip's calls have it there (`passing()`), so with a call leaving later, a tram a little past its stop looks less late, the Train is drawn running ahead of it, and waits for it at the next stop.
