# The timetable drives motion; live data only corrects it

No live feed is smooth enough to draw as-is: Renfe Cercanías pins stopped and arriving trains to station coordinates (36% of consecutive positions implied more than 180 km/h), FGC's data is 2–4 minutes old and refreshes every ~2 minutes, and the Metro publishes predictions only. So every Train moves along its Trip's track from the timetable, and live data only shifts it in time: its Delay comes from its position while it's Live and moving, and otherwise from the operator, or for Rodalies, the Cercanías núcleos and long distance from its last GPS, as Renfe's figure is often minutes off (#33, #260). Small disagreements are eased in (a Train slows or speeds up but never moves backwards); big ones snap. Live data also says how much of its Trip a Train runs: none where its operator says it won't run (Cancelled), and only part where it says the Train won't stop at some of its Stations, as Renfe says of one it cuts short or starts late. The timetable still drives it along that part (#346).

A Train its report has standing at a Station is held there: its Delay is never so small or so large that the timetable has it anywhere else when it was reported, where that Station is one its Trip calls at (#39). FGC's trip updates lag Geotren by a minute or two, and so had Trains leave Stations Geotren still had them standing at. Renfe's pinned Trains are the exception, and carry on from their last GPS Delay instead: Renfe pins Trains coming into a Station too, and its pinned Stations are stale, so holding them made their jumps worse (#8: 115 against 85), but for one its reports have named at one Station for 10 minutes or more, which stands there (#485, below). Nor are TRAM's held: the Station TRAM names is the one a Train is at or has just left, not one it stands at, and held there, a Train would stand 40–80 s and then jump a whole stretch, so it runs as late or early as TRAM says, which has most such Trains on their way (#42). A Train standing at its Trip's first Station can be there long before it leaves, off the map, so there it's only held from leaving: its Delay is never so small that the timetable has it gone when it was reported. The Metro's are drawn there all the same while their Block waits at the end of its Line, since the Block has just come in on the Trip before: a Live Metro Train short of its Trip's first Station, as TMB's time has it for as long as it waits, stands at that Station unless the Trip its Block ran in on is still on the map (#105). That's where it's drawn, not its Delay. Where live data knows no such Trip, as on a map just opened, it stands there at once, even while it's still running in: TMB's time can't tell the two apart. And it stays there until a report has it gone, by its Delay, when it was reported: while a Block waits, TMB's ETA for it keeps moving later, so a Train drawn leaving when an ETA said was drawn out on its track, where it can't run back, until the Block really left (#107). So it leaves a report or so late, a median half a minute over #31's replay, and catches up at up to line speed however far behind it's drawn, jumping only where live data has it over a kilometre on, or its Trip over. Held past when live data has it leave, or catching up, its times run as late as it's drawn, so it's leaving now in the follow panel, on its Station's board and nearby. Not one its timetable took out of that Station before live data first placed it there: that would run it back. Once out, it stays out: TMB's ETA for a Block that has left can move later again, as while it waited, and have it back at that Station, or behind where the Train is drawn on its first stretch. It then stands where it's drawn until its Delay catches up, a stall, rather than jumping back (#125). So that a Metro Train is seldom drawn out before its Block is known, a Scheduled one on a Line whose live data names Blocks, while that live data is available, waits at its first Station for its Block, for up to 3 minutes past when its timetable has it leave: drawn leaving on time, it snapped back as its Block was first listed for it, a minute or two late. Its Block's first report takes over there, and with none by then, it leaves as late as it waited and catches up with its timetable at up to line speed. Run that late to its end, one whose Block's reports had stopped was drawn 3 minutes back once the snapshots that placed it had gone. While it waits or catches up, its times run as late as it's drawn, as while it's held. L9's, L10's and the funicular's Trains keep to their timetable, as TMB publishes no predictions for them, and so does every Metro Train while the Metro's live data is unavailable.

A Train Renfe's reports have named at one Station, as `near`, for 10 minutes or more stands there, as FGC's do, until a report names another Station or gives GPS (#485). It's drawn at the Station, its times as late as it has stood there, however far its Delay had run it on. It's held from the 10 minutes, not before, as a pin of a minute or two says little (#8). Another Station or a GPS fix starts the 10 minutes again, and so does a gap of 2 minutes in its reports (`FOLLOWS`; 20 minutes instead has Rodalies' Live Train-minutes drawn far from a pin, below, at 96.7 and its jumps at 484); a report with no position between two doesn't. A Train named at its Trip's last Station isn't held: it has ended its run, and leaves the map as its timetable has it. One named at its first is only held from leaving, as FGC's. Both are as it runs the Trip: a Train Renfe cuts short or starts late, as with SKIPPED Stations (#346), has its last and its first where it stops. Once its pin ends a Train goes by its Delay as before, not the Delay it stood: one that stood jumps on to where its GPS Delay from before the pin has it, where it was drawn all along before the hold, until a fix puts it where it is. Why: of the 27 GPS fixes that came within 15 minutes after such a pin, in the four recordings of #233's audit (6, 7 and 10 October 2026), where the map had drawn the Train 3 km or more from it, 17 were at the pin, within 2 km, 7 near where the map had drawn the Train and 3 at neither. Over those four recordings, the Live Train-minutes drawn more than 3 km from a pin of 10 minutes or more (far minutes below) fell on Rodalies' from 353.4 to 98.7 (28.6, 70.4, 227.2 and 27.3 to 5.5, 18.2, 66.0 and 9.1: 223.6 for Wednesday's peak before #565), on Madrid's from 14.7 to 14.3 and on the other núcleos' from 10.8 to 9.7. What's left is the first 10 minutes of each run, which the hold leaves: 89.4 of Rodalies' 353.4, 14.3 of Madrid's 14.7 and 9.7 of the others' 10.8, so that of those after the 10 minutes, 264.0 became 9.3, 0.3 became 0 and 1.1 became 0. Renfe's jumps went from 467 to 485 on Rodalies' (+3.9%), from 1,927 to 1,823 on Madrid's and from 185 to 180 on the other núcleos'; Rodalies' are 17 Trains drawn on from their pin that snap back to it as the 10 minutes pass (30 over all seven recordings) and 9 within 3 minutes after a pin ends, 5 forward and 4 back where there were 2 forward and 7 back. Stalls of 30 s or more went from 41 to 41, from 527 to 439 and from 39 to 36, and the Live Train-seconds drawn 30 s or more ahead of live data from 1,509 to 1,512, 19,243 to 16,606 and 1,428 to 1,327, and behind it from 849 to 841, 1,961 to 1,947 and 576 to 537. Over all seven recordings, Rodalies' far minutes went from 495.3 to 147.8 and its jumps from 913 to 946 (+3.6%), its stalls, ahead and behind each down 1%. With the Stations the first frame of Renfe's trip updates lists as skipped put in every snapshot of the three weekdays, which they predate (#346), Rodalies' went from 28.6, 67.5 and 112.2 to 5.5, 18.1 and 37.7, and its jumps from 279 to 290 (+3.9%). FGC's, TRAM's and the Metro's are the same to the digit in all seven, and so is long distance's replay, whose reports name no Station.

Long distance's Trains run as their Line's speed profile has them over their Network's, so that a late MD never catches up faster than an MD runs, nor an AVE faster than 300 km/h: AVE y Larga Distancia runs at 250 km/h at 0.5 m/s², its AVE, Avlo and AVE Int at 300, and Media Distancia y Avant at Rodalies' 160 km/h at 1 m/s², its Avant and Avant Exp at 250 and 0.5 (#260). Renfe's long-distance positions are GPS whatever their `currentStatus` says: only 6 of 177 `STOPPED_AT` were within 500 m of their Station, so none holds a Train at a Station. They carry no time of their own, so the feed's header stands for it, and they freeze: 44% of consecutive polls repeat one, and 177 holds of a minute or more ended in jumps of over a kilometre, up to 73 km on 4 October 2026. So a Train carries on from its last GPS Delay as Rodalies' do, a position unchanged since its report before counting as none, and Renfe's own figure is only what there's no GPS Delay to carry, or where the GPS Delay no report supported is over 10 minutes from it (#565). A kilometre at 300 km/h is 12 s, less than the 20 s a Train takes to ease back, so a Train there is snapped where it's a minute off, as the time rule has it, and not a kilometre (`snap`). Over 12 minutes of 10 October 2026's long-distance data, 113 Trips traced crudely along OpenStreetMap's rails, that was 0.08 jumps for each Train-minute instead of 0.25, and 3.2% of its Live Train-seconds spent standing between Stations for 30 s or more, waiting for live data to catch up with where it was drawn, instead of 1.0%; for the 23 Trains whose position held and then moved, which the tests replay, 0.09 instead of 0.18. Of their 25 holds of a minute or more that ended in a move of a kilometre or more, none had a Train standing where its position was held, one snapped it back 1.8 km, the 72 s it lost while the position was held, two jumped on, by up to 21 km, and four, drawn ahead of where the new position had them, stood 21 to 48 s until live data caught up, where snapped a kilometre off one would have stood and two snapped back.

A GPS fix of Renfe's that no report of its Train within 2 minutes before could have run to is no position, and a GPS Delay no report before it supported goes only where Renfe's own figure was near it (#565). Renfe's GPS now and then has a Train where another is, or far off: on 7 October 2026 a C5's put it 23 minutes ahead and back at its next report. A Train's first GPS reports have none before them to be judged by, so such a fix was gone by: over #329's five recordings (6, 7 and 10 October), 65 of the 359 Delays that a Call took from a GPS Delay no report before it supported were 10 minutes or more early, 9 reports of 9 Trains (#410). So a fix further from each of the Train's last two GPS fixes within 2 minutes than it could have run in the time between them, at its Line's top speed and 5% over, as `jumps()` has it, and a second at the least, with 300 m more, is not a position; and a GPS Delay that was more than 10 minutes from Renfe's own figure in the report that gave it goes only once a later report agrees with it, and Renfe's figure till then. The fixes it judges by are the Train's last two as reported, taken as positions or not, and one reported again isn't another, so that a fix between two good ones doesn't take the good one after it with it. A report by Train number, as Renfe's long-distance files make, isn't judged: it has no time of its own, its feed's header stands for it, and its position holds for minutes and then moves kilometres, so the time between two says nothing of how far a Train could have run. Judged at their Line's top speed, 7 of the 228 fixes of 10 October 2026's long-distance replay would be none, and each is its Train's real position, its Delay within 5 minutes of Renfe's figure: four would leave their Train drawn 3 to 16 km behind for 40 s to 5 minutes, one of them 8 km behind for 5 minutes and then jumping 7.9 km on, and one Train's stale first position, which comes back twice, would be taken as a position in place of its two real fixes. The gate goes by the figure of the report that gave the GPS Delay, not the latest: a position holds while Renfe's figure goes on, and the Train would be drawn by the figure from 10 minutes on where its GPS had it in its place, as above. On #329's five recordings the Delays from such a GPS Delay that were 10 minutes or more early fell from 65 of 359 to 6 of 293 (2%), and over them and the two held out, Renfe's jumps from 4,597 to 4,487, stalls of 30 s or more from 998 to 972 and the Live Train-seconds drawn 30 s or more ahead of live data from 36,509 to 36,251, with those drawn 30 s or more behind it up from 6,448 to 6,612 (2.5%). On the two held out alone, those ahead went from 11,133 to 11,203 (0.6%): 70 Train-seconds net of 49 Trains' changes, 25 up by 526 s and 24 down by 456 s, the largest 120 s of one Bilbao Train's, whose GPS sat at one point.

## Considered Options

- **Plot live positions directly**, as markers that jump from report to report. Rejected: trains would teleport between stations, freeze for minutes at a time, and Networks without positions would show no Trains at all.
- **Snapping a long-distance Train where it's a kilometre off, as Rodalies' is, or 20 s off at its top speed, about Rodalies' kilometre in time (22.5 s at 160 km/h).** Rejected (#260, the maintainer's pick, 10 October 2026): over the same 12 minutes they made 0.25 and 0.16 jumps for each Train-minute against 0.08, for 1.0% and 1.3% of its Live Train-seconds standing between Stations against 3.2%. A minute is what the time rule already calls big, and the only one of the three under #33's 0.1 jumps for each Train-minute for Rodalies.
- **Renfe's own figure, instead of a GPS Delay no report before it supported, where none has counted in the last half hour (`delayBy()`).** Rejected (#410, 11 October 2026). Over #329's five recordings (6, 7 and 10 October), 65 of the 359 Delays that a Call took from such a GPS Delay were 10 minutes or more early. They were 9 reports, one each of 9 Trains, each among its Train's first GPS fixes in the recording, so that none had counted: 6 carry exactly another Trip's position from the same snapshot, 3 (2 of the 6 among them) are 3.8 to 34 km off their Trip's track, one is on its track with no other report there, and one is a Train whose own figure was 34 minutes early. Going by the figure for them left 5 of 463 Delays 10 minutes or more early, all one Train's, but drew more Trains ahead of their GPS and then standing: Renfe's figure moves in whole minutes (#33) and is under its GPS far more often than over it (#329), and 22 of Rodalies' 23 new stalls of 30 s or more, and 116 of Madrid's 123, were a Train standing 40 s or so while the figure it went by stepped up a minute. Rodalies' stalls of 30 s or more went from 46 to 67 and Cercanías Madrid's from 605 to 717, Madrid's jumps from 2,078 to 2,198, and the Live Train-seconds drawn 30 s or more ahead of live data from 25,376 to 33,014 across Renfe's Networks, with Rodalies' 0.075 jumps for each Train-minute unchanged; on 10 October's other two recordings, Madrid's jumps went from 933 to 1,076. What took them out is the rule above (#565): taking a fix no report of the Train within two minutes could have run to as no position took 6 of the 9 out, leaving 26 of 320 (8%), and with the gate, 6 of 293 (2%).
- **A GPS fix more than 1 km from its Trip's track is no position** (`OFF_TRACK` for every report, not those by Train number alone, which #486's third bullet asks of 500 m). Not built (#410, #565): it took 3 of the 9 reports out, 65 of the Delays 10 minutes or more early to 45, and left jumps, stalls and the Trains drawn ahead where they were: 2,874, 701 and 25,376 to 2,866, 705 and 25,514 on #329's five recordings.
- **The rule without the gate, or the gate without the rule** (#565). The rule alone leaves 26 of 320 such Delays 10 minutes or more early (8%), over the 5% that #410 was held to, and is better than going without on jumps (2,874 to 2,799), stalls of 30 s or more (701 to 670) and the Trains drawn ahead (25,376 to 24,597), and a little worse on those drawn behind (3,675 to 3,733), over the five. The gate alone leaves 6 of 288 (2%), and is barely better than going without on jumps (2,874 to 2,859) and worse on the rest: 711 stalls, 25,739 Trains drawn ahead and 3,725 behind. Together they have the 6 of 293 and 2,803, 680, 25,048 and 3,792, at the cost against the rule alone of 451 Live Train-seconds drawn ahead and 59 behind.
- **The gate by Renfe's latest figure**, as #410 measured it, a GPS Delay going only while it's within 10 minutes of the figure the Train's latest report has. Not built (#565): on the two recordings held out it had the Live Train-seconds drawn 30 s or more ahead of live data 3.5% over going without (11,133 to 11,523, against 11,203 by the report's own figure), and a long-distance Train whose GPS held while Renfe's figure grew a minute every 40 s, as 4 October 2026's longest did for 26 minutes, was drawn by the figure once that was 10 minutes from its GPS Delay, 21 km back where its GPS had it in its place all along (`engine.longDistance.test.ts`).
- **One top speed for every Line**, 60 m/s, as #410's sweep had it. Not built (#565): it's over every Cercanías' and Rodalies' 120 to 160 km/h, so that it let through fixes that no Train of Rodalies' could have run to at 46.7 m/s with the 5%, or of most núcleos' at 35 (2,812 jumps, 672 stalls and 24,671 Trains drawn ahead on #329's five recordings, against 2,799, 670 and 24,597 by the Line's, both without the gate), and it's under an AVE's 83 m/s.
- **A Train held goes on from the Delay it stood once its pin ends**, as late as it was when its last pin was reported, in place of its GPS Delay from before, which is stale after a stand, or Renfe's figure. Not built (#485), for the maintainer's pick: it's the better estimate, but it moves Rodalies' jumps past the 5% the hold was held to. Of the 16 pins of 10 minutes or more at a Station other than a Trip's first that a GPS fix followed within 15 minutes, the Delay the fix measured was nearest the Delay the Train stood in all 16, a median 83 s off, against 560 s for the last GPS Delay before the pin (13 of the 16 had one) and 435 s for Renfe's figure; at a first Station in 15 of 19, 118 s against the figure's 247 s. Built, jumps are 485 on Rodalies' four recordings, 1,823 on Madrid's and 180 on the other núcleos'; with it, 493 (+5.6%), 1,721 (-11%) and 167 (-10%), its stalls 42, 430 and 33, and its drawn-ahead seconds 1,540, 16,112 and 1,208. It has to run out sooner than a GPS Delay does, CARRY: a Delay from a pin of 10 minutes depends on 10 minutes of reports before it, so the snapshots kept (KEEP) can carry it for 24 minutes, not 30, or replaying them anew draws a Train another way than folding each in did.
- **Another length for the pin than 10 minutes** (the maintainer's pick of 11 October 2026). On the same four recordings Rodalies' jumps went from 467 to 936 (+100%) at 3 minutes and to 634 (+36%) at 5, with 44.5 far minutes at 5, and to 509 (+9%) at 15, with 150.5 far minutes, 57% fewer: at 10 minutes, 485 and 98.7.
- **A minimum dwell at TRAM's calls (20 s, 30 s): a call's departure no earlier than its arrival plus the dwell, so long as the quickest run to the next stop still fits, for TRAM's Trips only.** Rejected (#405, 10 October 2026). Over #344's replay of 7 October's two weekday recordings, stalls of 30 s or more (a Live Train standing still, more than 20 m from every call, for 30 s or more) went from 64 to 85 to 135 at 14:03 and from 88 to 121 to 162 at 17:47, and the Train-seconds drawn more than 5 s ahead of their reports from 18,658 to 20,425 to 22,329 and from 24,547 to 26,730 to 28,937. Jumps forward and back went from 14 and 11 to 17 and 13 to 24 and 19 at 14:03, and from 16 and 15 to 16 and 18 to 22 and 20 at 17:47. Moving a call's arrival earlier by up to the dwell, and keeping its departure, did no better: 74 and 78 stalls at 14:03, 95 and 96 at 17:47. Why, as far as read and not measured: `delayOf()` takes a tram's Delay from where its report puts it along its Trip, against when the Trip's calls have it there (`passing()`), so with a call leaving later, a tram a little past its stop looks less late, the Train is drawn running ahead of it, and waits for it at the next stop.
