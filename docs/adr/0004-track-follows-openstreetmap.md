# Track follows OpenStreetMap's rails, not the feeds' shapes

The operators' shapes don't sit on the rails the map draws. Between Terrassa and Manresa, Renfe's run about 1.5 m off OpenStreetMap's rails (up to about 5 m), with a point every 46 m or so. Each Line is traced separately, so R4 and RL4 weave around each other on the track they share, and some shapes are coarse: RT2's has 19 points. The basemap is drawn from OpenStreetMap, so the daily build traces each shape along OpenStreetMap's rail network instead. It snaps the Stations the shape's Trips serve to the network, in order, and between consecutive ones takes the shortest path a train can over the Network's own kind of rails: it turns back only at one of those Stations (as R16's Trains from Tortosa do at L'Aldea to go on to Ulldecona, where Renfe's shape turns at the switch short of it: #63), never at a switch, keeps to one track rather than cross over to save a metre or two, and prefers the track the Lines traced before it take the same way. Lines that share track going the same way then share one geometry, on the rails people see.

Each way a shape's Trips run it is traced on its own (#25): where they run it backwards, as every R7 Trip does, it's traced reversed, as `<id>:back`, and a shape run both ways is traced once each way. On double track a trace keeps to its Network's running side, so Trains going opposite ways run on the tracks they really use; where more tracks run side by side they pair off from one side, and each way keeps to its side of its pair. Lines share a track's geometry only when they run it the same way: single track, and double track mapped as one way, stay shared. Every Network keeps right. In Spain the old Norte company's lines run on the left and MZA's on the right; Catalonia's one Norte line on the left, Manresa–Barcelona, changed to the right in December 1971 (García Álvarez, *La vía doble en España y el sentido de circulación de los trenes por ella*, Fundación de los Ferrocarriles Españoles, 2010). OpenStreetMap's `railway:preferred_direction` tags agree: they have Adif's Iberian-gauge Trains on the right on 94% of the 268 km of double track they cover in Catalonia, and FGC's, TRAM's and the Metro's on 96–99% of theirs, which FGC's live positions bear out. That overturns the assumption in #25 that Adif's conventional lines run on the left. The Metro's L2 runs on the left between Tetuan and Paral·lel, so where those tags say which way Trains run a track, a trace follows them rather than its Network's side. This replaces the v1 spec's plan (issue #1) to use the shapes and borrow missing sections from other Lines. railisland traces Taiwan's railways the same way; we take the idea only (ADR-0001).

## Considered Options

- **Keep the feeds' shapes.** Rejected: the offsets only show zoomed right in, but that's where people follow a Train, and Lines on shared track would still weave.
- **Move each shape point to the nearest OpenStreetMap rail.** Rejected: at junctions, and where lines run side by side, the nearest rail is often another line's, and the feeds' gaps and coarse stretches stay.

## Consequences

- The daily build downloads OpenStreetMap's rails for Catalonia from Overpass, and those within 10 km beyond its land border, so a Line's track reaches the first Station beyond it (#62). The map already credits OpenStreetMap contributors.
- Trips keep their shapes, or run the reversed one where they run theirs backwards; the bundle names each Network's running side and each shape's sides, so the map can put Trains on their Line's stroke zoomed out, a little to their running side, where a double track's two tracks fall on one pixel.
- A shape runs from the first to the last Station its Trips serve: beyond the feed's shape where Trips run further, which fills its gaps, and short of it where none do, so RL4 starts at Manresa. A Station on a branch off the middle of the feed's shape, such as Estació de França for R2N, is left out rather than traced out to and back. The Trips that call there can't be placed on their shape, so the build leaves them out of the bundle and reports them.
- A stretch that can't be traced keeps the feed's shape, and the build reports it. It also reports where a trace turns back on the way from one Station to the next, which missing rails could cause too. OpenStreetMap can be wrong too (railisland found a stretch mapped on an old alignment 800 m away), so where the feed has the track, a traced shape much longer or shorter than the feed's fails the build.
