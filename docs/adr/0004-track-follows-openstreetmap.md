# Track follows OpenStreetMap's rails, not the feeds' shapes

The operators' shapes don't sit on the rails the map draws. Between Terrassa and Manresa, Renfe's run about 1.5 m off OpenStreetMap's rails (up to about 5 m), with a point every 46 m or so. Each Line is traced separately, so R4 and RL4 weave around each other on the track they share, and some shapes are coarse: RT2's has 19 points. The basemap is drawn from OpenStreetMap, so the daily build traces each shape along OpenStreetMap's rail network instead. It snaps the Stations the shape's Trips serve to the network, in order, and between consecutive ones takes the shortest path a train can over the Network's own kind of rails: it turns back only at one of those Stations (as R16's Trains from Tortosa do at L'Aldea to go on to Ulldecona, where Renfe's shape turns at the switch short of it: #63), never at a switch, keeps to one track rather than cross over to save a metre or two, and prefers the track the Lines traced before it take the same way. Lines that share track going the same way then share one geometry, on the rails people see.

Each way a shape's Trips run it is traced on its own (#25): where they run it backwards, as every R7 Trip does, it's traced reversed, as `<id>:back`, and a shape run both ways is traced once each way. On double track a trace keeps to its Network's running side, so Trains going opposite ways run on the tracks they really use; where more tracks run side by side they pair off from one side, and each way keeps to its side of its pair. Lines share a track's geometry only when they run it the same way: single track, and double track mapped as one way, stay shared. A Network keeps right, unless all its double track keeps left. In Spain the old Norte company's lines run on the left and MZA's on the right; Catalonia's one Norte line on the left, Manresa–Barcelona, changed to the right in December 1971 (García Álvarez, *La vía doble en España y el sentido de circulación de los trenes por ella*, Fundación de los Ferrocarriles Españoles, 2010). OpenStreetMap's `railway:preferred_direction` tags agree: they have Adif's Iberian-gauge Trains on the right on 94% of the 268 km of double track they cover in Catalonia, and FGC's, TRAM's and the Metro's on 96–99% of theirs, which FGC's live positions bear out. That overturns the assumption in #25 that Adif's conventional lines run on the left. The Metro's L2 runs on the left between Tetuan and Paral·lel, Cercanías Madrid on the old Norte line from Pinar de las Rozas on to Villalba and El Escorial (García Álvarez, table 3), and Cercanías Santander's C2 and C3 on FEVE's metre-gauge lines, which keep left too, so where those tags say which way Trains run a track, a trace follows them rather than its Network's side (#248). Cercanías San Sebastián runs on the Norte lines that still keep left (García Álvarez, table 4), Bilbao on those and FEVE's, and Cartagena, Ferrol and León on FEVE's alone, so they keep left; OpenStreetMap tags none of their double track. Cercanías Asturias runs on such lines alone too, but keeps right until six ways of its left-hand track between Villallana and Mieres have a gauge in OpenStreetMap: without them, C1's trace kept to the left turns back to Pola de Lena and comes out longer than the feed's shape, which fails the build (#253). This replaces the v1 spec's plan (issue #1) to use the shapes and borrow missing sections from other Lines. railisland traces Taiwan's railways the same way; we take the idea only (ADR-0001).

## Considered Options

- **Keep the feeds' shapes.** Rejected: the offsets only show zoomed right in, but that's where people follow a Train, and Lines on shared track would still weave.
- **Move each shape point to the nearest OpenStreetMap rail.** Rejected: at junctions, and where lines run side by side, the nearest rail is often another line's, and the feeds' gaps and coarse stretches stay.

## Consequences

- The daily build takes OpenStreetMap's rails from Geofabrik's extracts of Spain and of the French regions along Catalonia's border (ADR-0009), which reach beyond Spain's border, where the build cuts the Lines (ADR-0010), so a Line's track reaches the first Station beyond it (#62). The map already credits OpenStreetMap contributors.
- Trips keep their shapes, or run the reversed one where they run theirs backwards; the bundle names each Network's running side and where each shape's Trains go on their Line's stroke (its `slots`, #176), so the map can put them there zoomed out, a little to their running side, where a double track's two tracks fall on one pixel.
- A shape runs from the first to the last Station its Trips serve: beyond the feed's shape where Trips run further, which fills its gaps, and short of it where none do, so RL4 starts at Manresa. A Station on a branch off the middle of the feed's shape, such as Estació de França for R2N, is left out rather than traced out to and back. The Trips that call there can't be placed on their shape, so the build leaves them out of the bundle and reports them.
- A timetable with no shapes, as Renfe's long-distance one, is traced through the Stations of each run of its Trips, a Line's Trips that call at the same Stations, on the rails of their gauge (#259): AVE's, Avlo's, AVE Int's and Avant's on standard gauge, and on Iberian gauge too where a stretch has no path on standard gauge alone; Alvia's, Euromed's, Intercity's and Trencelta's on both; MD's and the regionals' on Iberian gauge, and the ex-FEVE regionals' on metre gauge. A way with no gauge tag is every gauge's, and a Train changes gauge only at a node tagged `railway=gauge_conversion`, so its trace runs on each gauge as a layer of track that only a changer joins to another. At a Station it can set off from another of its tracks of the gauge it came in on, as where OpenStreetMap has no switch between them. With no feed's shape to hold it against, a hop traced more than twice its straight line and 10 km more is logged, but fails nothing, and a run with a Station off the rails, or a stretch with no path, isn't drawn straight across the country: its Trips are left out and logged. Its Trains are cut at Spain's border before they're traced, keeping the first call beyond it, so AVE Int is traced to Perpignan. A Station published away from its rails is moved by its point, in its timetable's config, as Antequera AV is, 430 m off.
- A stretch that can't be traced keeps the feed's shape, and the build reports it. It also reports where a trace turns back on the way from one Station to the next, which missing rails could cause too. OpenStreetMap can be wrong too (railisland found a stretch mapped on an old alignment 800 m away), so where the feed has the track, a traced shape much longer or shorter than the feed's fails the build.
- OpenStreetMap maps a curve as straight lengths between its points, which zoomed in show as facets. From zoom 15, where the map draws each Line on its own rails, the page rounds each bend into an arc no more than 2 m off the rails as it draws them, and has MapLibre simplify those rails less than it does by default, which would facet the arcs again (#203). Rounding in the daily build instead would make the track file about a quarter larger. Trains still run on the shape as traced, so at a bend one sits up to 2 m outside the arc. Shapes are written to six decimals, as at five, about a metre, OpenStreetMap's own close-set points zig-zag; to pay for it, the build first drops the points of each way that are less than 0.3 m off its line, under a pixel at zoom 18.
