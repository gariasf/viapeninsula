# Live data is a snapshot file on R2, not an API

One Cloudflare Durable Object wakes about every 20 seconds, fetches each live feed as often as its source allows (Renfe's Cercanías files and TRAM every time, Renfe's long-distance files and the Metro every other time, FGC and Euskotren every 2 minutes; TRAM waits up to 30 minutes after it answers with no access token or refuses the one it issued, but not after no answer or an HTTP status of 500 or more), and writes one compact JSON snapshot to R2, and beside it, when they change, the operators' Alerts in a file of their own (below); viewers fetch those files through Cloudflare's CDN on a custom domain. Viewer traffic never runs our code, so cost doesn't grow with viewers and abuse can't raise the bill (their cache key ignores query strings).

## Considered Options

- **A Worker that runs on every viewer request, with an edge cache.** Rejected: every poll is billed, cache hits included, the free plan covers only about 23 viewers polling all day, and it would need rate limiting.
- **A fetcher on a home server (Umbrel) or a Hetzner machine.** Kept only as the fallback for any feed that can't be fetched within Cloudflare's limits, for example if Cloudflare's shared outgoing IPs trip FGC's quota of 5,000 requests a day per IP.

## Consequences

- The snapshot records each Network's freshness: that of the feed that covers it, which one feed can give many Networks, as Renfe's Cercanías files give Rodalies and Cercanías Madrid theirs (#248). That is what turns Trains Scheduled and raises the "live data unavailable" banner, and a feed that fails raises it for every Network it covers, though the banner names each only while its Trains are in view (ADR-0010). The map raises it too where a feed works but has had none of its Network's Trains in it for about three of its updates (five, for a Network whose feed covers others too, but for Renfe's long-distance one's: below) while the timetable has at least 5 of them on the map, as on 28 September 2026, when Renfe served its Cercanías feeds with a header and no Trains (#124). The snapshot still says `ok` for that feed, since the fetcher never loads the timetable, so the banner and the maintainer's checks on the snapshot no longer go by one signal alone.
- Where a feed that covers several Networks works but has none of one Network's Trains in it, the fetcher keeps that Network's last reports for two more tries, and drops them on the third. Renfe's Cercanías files drop all of Madrid's Trains now and then, with a fresh header and every other núcleo's: in 5 of 26 fetches on 4 October 2026, and 15 of 154 on the morning of 5 October, never more than two in a row. The map hears the kept reports, so for those Networks the banner for a feed with none of their Trains comes two tries later, and a Train Renfe pins to a Station stays Live through them. One placed by its GPS doesn't: the same position again counts as none (#33), so it still turns Scheduled after three missed updates. A feed that covers one Network can't drop it but by dropping all its Trains, which the map tells on its own (#124), so the fetcher keeps nothing for it. Nor does it keep any of Renfe's long-distance files' reports, which name a Train by its number alone, not by a Trip, and so not by its Network: AVE y Larga Distancia's and Media Distancia y Avant's banner comes at the third of those files' updates without their Trains (#262). Each try that finds a Network missing, up to the one that drops its reports, goes in the fetcher's logs, which Workers Logs keeps for 7 days, to count how often (#248).
- Renfe's long-distance live data, `vehicle_positions_LD.json` and `trip_updates_LD.json` (no key, CC BY 4.0), is a source of its own, fetched every other run: its headers advance every 14–30 s, so a try 20 s after the last could find them not updated since, and fail. Its freshness is AVE y Larga Distancia's and Media Distancia y Avant's, and its report of one of their Trains, by its number, is their own feed's, which keeps their live data available while their Trains are in it (#124, #262). It names any part of a Train's run, by a trip_id whose date is its timetable's, not the Train's day, and lists a Train once for each part, so the fetcher names each Train by its Train number (`vehicle.label`, the trip_id's first five digits), in one report with its parts' positions and trip updates merged, and the map takes it as the Trip of that number, in any Network, whose timetable runs nearest when it was reported. So Trains kept from Rodalies' or a núcleo's timetable are Live from these files too, as R15 17050, which only they report (#258, #261): from 13:47 to 13:59 on 10 October 2026, 24–26 of their 112–115 Trains matched a Trip on the map, 11–16 of them running then. A Train its own Network's feed reports too, 6–10 a file then, goes by that feed's report. One they alone report goes by their feed's freshness, not its Trip's Network's (#425): it's Live within three of their updates, 120 s, and while their feed fails, their last report of it, which the fetcher keeps, is as old as their last good file, so that the Train turns Scheduled and drops its Delay after 30 minutes, as one whose own feed fails does. Over #261's 12 minutes of recordings, the Trains they placed flipped between Live and Scheduled 16 times where they had 26, against 12 with Renfe's Cercanías files alone, and with those failing from 6 minutes in, R16 18127 stayed Live for 546 s of 671 where it was for 275. And where its GPS gives no Delay, off its Trip's track, before it leaves its first Station, or the same GPS as their report before, their report says nothing of a Rodalies or núcleo Trip, which is drawn as without it: Renfe's figure is against their own timetable, which runs R15 17050 30 minutes after Rodalies' does, so that the Train was drawn Live 20–25 km ahead of its GPS by it on 10 October 2026 (#442). Their own Networks' Trips still go by that figure there. The positions are GPS whatever their currentStatus says, as Renfe doesn't pin these Trains to Stations, and as of the files' headers, as they give no time of their own; one over a kilometre from its Trip's track is on a part of the run the Trip doesn't cover, and measures no Delay. A Train is Cancelled only where every trip update for its number is CANCELED and it has no position: Renfe lists CANCELED trip updates after running ones for Trains it places, as for 17501 and Intercity 01460. And only once its Trip is due to leave its first Station: such an update has no stops, and its trip_id's date is its calendar's, so it can't say which run, and until then the Train can run after all, as FEVE's 71832, CANCELED alone from 13:47 on 10 October 2026, ran out of Oviedo from 14:45 (#429). Their SKIPPED Stations are carried by Train number (below). They add 10.5–10.8 KB to the snapshot, 1.5 KB by brotli.
- Euskotren's live data is its GTFS-RT trip updates on Moveuskadi, `gtfsrt_euskotren_trip_updates.pb` (no key, CC BY 4.0, no rate limit stated on its dataset, legal or API pages, nor in any header), read by a generic `gtfs-rt` adapter: its positions file is empty. It's fetched every sixth run, every 2 minutes, as its header moves every 2 minutes exactly (19 samples on 4 October 2026), so a faster fetch would fail every other try as not updated and double the download, to about 320 MB a day. The file lists about 300 Trips, most yet to start, and its trams' and the funicular's: the fetcher keeps its trains' Trips running as of their updates, each by the Station it comes to next and when it's expected there, as the Metro's are (#265). It stalled for 8 hours on 24 September 2026; it needs nothing of its own for that: after 3 missed updates the map turns its Trains Scheduled and raises the banner, as for any feed.
- The snapshot also carries the Stations Renfe's trip updates list a Trip's Train won't stop at (SKIPPED), by the Trip, and when Renfe first said so of any, which the map cuts a Train short or starts it late by (#346). Renfe lists only the Stations a Trip's timetable hasn't left yet, and drops a Trip once its Train has ended its run, so the fetcher keeps them for 3 hours after Renfe last listed the Trip. A Station Renfe lists again, not SKIPPED, is no longer skipped: on 7 October 2026 it took back C4b's four from Las Margaritas to Parla one by one as the Train ran on to them. In that day's rains they added at most 2.9 KB to the snapshot, 260 bytes compressed; on an ordinary day Rodalies has none, and Cercanías Madrid a few. Renfe's long-distance files list each part of a Train's run with the Stations its timetable hasn't left yet, so theirs are kept by Train number, the union over the number's parts, and kept as a Trip's are; the map cuts the Trip of that number, in any Network, whose timetable runs nearest when Renfe first said so, as a report by number is matched, unless the Trip's own Network's feed has listed Stations it skips, which go first (#426, #261). From 13:47 to 13:59 on 10 October 2026 they were Intercity 01460's, cut short at Elche/Elx-Parc, and R17 15062's, at Vilanova i la Geltrú, which Renfe's Cercanías files listed none SKIPPED for: 212 bytes a snapshot, 13–53 by brotli.
- The snapshot carries, for a Rodalies or núcleo Train Renfe names at a Station (`near`), when Renfe first named it there (`since`, the first such report's time) and whether Renfe has it STOPPED_AT or INCOMING_AT it (`status`, its `currentStatus`) (#670). The map holds a Train named at one Station for 10 minutes or more there (ADR-0002, #485), and a page has only the snapshots since it opened, each with a Train's latest report alone, so it counted the 10 minutes from its own first snapshot: a Train Renfe had named at a Station for 40 minutes when a viewer opened the map was drawn by its Delay for 10 more, and then snapped back to the Station. The fetcher counts them instead, in Renfe's adapter's own state, kept between runs as its skipped Stations are: another Station or a GPS fix starts a Train's count again, a report with no position doesn't, and nor does a run that leaves it out, until 2 minutes have passed since the last that had it, the gap the map judged before (`FOLLOWS`). The map goes by a report's `since` where it has one, and counts from its own first report where it hasn't, as a snapshot from before then. Replayed on #233's four recordings with the fetcher's count run over every snapshot from each one's start, a page opened 15 minutes in drew Rodalies' Trains more than 3 km from a pin that had already stood 10 minutes for 1.1 Live Train-minutes where it drew them for 98.5, and opened 30 minutes in, 1.4 where 106.1; Renfe's jumps fell on each (Rodalies' 371 to 357 and 206 to 190). A page opened at a recording's first snapshot is the same either way, as the fetcher has no history from before it there. The map doesn't hold a Train every report of whose pin has `status` INCOMING_AT (ADR-0002, #671). They add about 22 bytes for each Train named at a Station, 40 to 170 of them a snapshot in those recordings: 0.9 to 3.7 KB raw, 72 to 282 bytes by brotli.
- From 10 to 13 October 2026 the snapshot carried, for a TRAM Train whose trip update had it at a stop it had reached and was still to leave, which stop, by its number in its Trip, and when it was due out (#344, PR #407). It no longer does: replayed on 13 October's three Record runs, holding a Train by it put it further from its tram, and holding only the late ones (due out after their timetable) gained nothing (below), so the map never read it and the fetcher was taken back to reading TRAM's trip updates only to the first stop with a time, as FGC's. What it found stays here and in `docs/research/live-data-sources.md`. TRAM's Delay stays what it was as the Train arrived, and its position names the stop it's at or has just left, so nothing else of TRAM's says a stop reached is still to be left. That isn't that the tram stands: for one that came early it's its timetable's departure, which its trams don't wait for (`docs/research/live-data-sources.md`), so the map didn't read it until a Record run with it in the snapshots said for which Trains a hold by it was right. The fetcher read every stop of each of TRAM's trip updates, keeping the last the Train had reached. In the frame of 25 September 2026, 11:44:50, 7 of TRAM's 24 Trains had one, which added 319 bytes to the 2,325 of its TRAM-only snapshot, 59 gzipped (39 by brotli), about 46 raw and 8 gzipped for each Train standing. On 13 October (10:49, 13:08 and 14:58, 45 minutes each), held by it, TRAM's jumps rose from 30, 8 and 3 to 96, 105 and 115, its stalls of 30 s or more from 60, 52 and 54 to 91, 101 and 116, and an early tram was drawn a median 374, 327 and 379 m from the stop it was next read at, where 129, 106 and 150 m without it; held only where it was due out after its timetable (205, 164 and 129 of 845, 1,093 and 1,293 reports), its jumps were the same, its stalls 51, 55 and 54, and a late tram as far from its next stop as without (median 117, 98 and 93 m), with 18 to 23 held Trains drawn more than 100 m from their stop.
- The operators' Alerts (ADR-0012) are a second file, `alerts.json`, and not in the snapshot: they rarely change, and the page keeps every snapshot for 35 minutes. Renfe's are asked for on every run with a conditional request, which Renfe answers with 304 and no body while its file is unchanged, as it was for hours on 7 October 2026, so a file that stays the same is no failure there, though it is one for live data (#35, #49). TRAM's are asked for every 5 minutes, for each half, with the access token its adapter keeps, and never ask for one, so a refusal or a failure there never holds TRAM's live data back (#43, #51, #128). The file says when each feed was last read, when its file last changed and how its last try went, and keeps a feed's last Alerts through tries that fail. It's written whenever a feed's Alerts change or a try at it starts or stops failing, and at least every 5 minutes, so that the times it gives for each feed, when it was last read and when its file last changed, are never more than that behind, and it's cached for a minute, as the page reads it once a minute (#339). A file's time is Renfe's Last-Modified, which its 304s keep, or the file's header timestamp where a feed gives none, the later of TRAM's two halves'. It's there because Renfe's file stood unchanged from 7 October 2026, 09:32 CEST, for days, and every 304 is a try that worked, so the time it was read couldn't say so: a card says when the file last changed where that's over a day before the map's time. The stuck-file rule stays clear of it, as a file that stands for hours is no failure (#390).
- It needs a domain on Cloudflare: R2's `r2.dev` URLs aren't cached and are rate-limited.
- It runs on Workers Paid ($5/month): a fetcher run measured about 12 ms of CPU, over the free plan's 10 ms. Viewer traffic never reaches a Worker, so usage stays inside the plan's included amounts.
- The Durable Object is evicted between alarms, so its throttles, tokens and each feed's last good reports live in its storage, not in memory (measured in the Cloudflare spike, see `docs/research/live-data-sources.md`).
- The Durable Object is made in Western Europe, by a location hint, near the live sources, all of them in Spain (#803). Cloudflare had put the first one, made with no hint, about 159 ms from Madrid, about as far as Seattle, where Euskadi's servers didn't answer it: none of Euskotren's 1,223 tries worked in its first 41 hours. Timed on 16 October 2026 from Durable Objects placed in each region, Euskotren's feed answered every try from the EU's, in Madrid, Lisbon, Marseille, Milan, Frankfurt, Prague, Vienna and Warsaw, in under 1.5 s, while tries hung for over 15 s from Chicago, Seattle, Singapore and Hong Kong, and Tokyo's and Sydney's got HTTP 520s. The EU jurisdiction was considered, which Cloudflare keeps to where it takes a hint as a preference, but workerd doesn't implement it, so the fetcher couldn't run locally. A hint counts only when an object is made, so the fetcher has a new name, `fetcher-weur`, and the first one deletes what it kept at its next alarm, and sets no next run.
- The fetcher runs in a Worker of its own, with no routes, rather than in the site's. A script in the site's Worker would run for every request that matches none of the site's files, and anyone can send those.
