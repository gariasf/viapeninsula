# Lines side by side follow a line graph

Where Lines share track, or run on tracks too close together to tell apart zoomed out, the map draws them side by side, a line width apart, as a transit map does. Until now `sideBySide()` offset each Line from its own track, and chose its side for each 50 m piece of that track, a Line at a time, from the Lines within NEAR of the piece. The study for #138 ([Lines side by side](../research/lines-side-by-side.md)) traced most of the faults it found to that: a Line drawn twice, where its two directions have tracks of their own and get different sides; steps and gaps, where a neighbour's track comes in and out of NEAR; and Lines crossing each other inside a bundle, because the offsets from neighbouring tracks don't nest. Tuning NEAR, SHORT or #159's ALONG moved those faults rather than removing them: #159 halved the breaks and drew Lines off a track they have alone for about 28 km. So the daily build draws Lines side by side from a line graph, as LOOM does (Bast, Brosi and Storandt, SIGSPATIAL 2018 and ACM TSAS 2019). Each edge is a stretch: a length of track, or of tracks within NEAR of each other and running alongside, that one set of Lines takes, drawn along one centreline averaged from its tracks. A node sits wherever that set changes. Every Line on a stretch is offset from its centreline by its place in the stretch's order, and it counts once on it, whichever way and whichever of its tracks it runs.

The graph is built from the traced shapes (ADR-0004), which stay the Trips' track: only how the Lines are drawn changes. Tracks join a stretch as they join a piece's cluster now, within NEAR and running alongside, with LOOM's tolerance for short excursions beyond it. Short stretches merge into their neighbours on the graph, which replaces SHORT and #159's `steady()`. Only Lines on the same level share a stretch (#165): not where one runs in a tunnel or on a bridge and the other doesn't, or their OpenStreetMap `layer` differs, as a tram on the avenue over the Rodalies tunnel at Glòries. The trace keeps each track's level from its tags, leaving out bridges and tunnels under 150 m, which tracks alongside each other are often mapped crossing at different places; and a Line's own two tracks go together whatever their levels, as their tunnels are often mapped at different layers, or one longer. Past six Lines side by side, the gap between them narrows, so that a stretch gets no wider than six Lines are; tracks nearby don't move aside for it. Where a Line on another level runs within a stretch's width, it's drawn on its own track, by depth: Lines in tunnels below those on the street, the deeper the lower, and where one above covers one, it shows through, dashed, but not along a stretch past six Lines, whose Lines cover each other (#178).

## Considered Options

- **Keep offsetting each Line from its own track, and tune the thresholds.** Rejected: every tuning the study tried traded one fault for another. SHORT at 800 m halved the breaks and drew Lines on top of each other for 3.5 km; #159's ALONG kept Lines beside a bundle they had left.
- **Draw each track once, and never offset (railisland's way).** Rejected: zoomed out, shared track is one pixel, and Lines side by side are what make the map read as a transit map.
- **Simplify or smooth the geometry the map is given.** Rejected: a GeoJSON tolerance of 2 px trades folds on tight curves for corners and slivers, and does nothing for the other faults.
- **Build the graph in the page.** Rejected: the graph is the same all day, the build has the time, and the page only draws.

## Consequences

- The build gains a stage between tracing and drawing. It replaces `sideBySide()` a step at a time: the graph, with today's order and a stroke per stretch for each Line on it (#161); one order per stretch, from LOOM's integer program, with crossings at forks rather than mid-stretch (#162: at the Station before a fork, #160's breaks count each as a swap and two steps, so the maintainer chose forks); curves across nodes instead of steps (#163: made in the build for each zoom band, a cubic Bézier between the two Stretches' centrelines, drawn with `line-offset` eased from one side to the other, so that its ends meet the strokes at every zoom in its band, which a curve from port to port wouldn't, as ports are px off the centreline; the maintainer chose this); centrelines smoothed at the scale of their offset (#164: made in the build for each zoom band, where a Line drawn off a centreline would fold at the band's zoom, by a gaussian blur widened until none does, as the map simplifies each stroke; it replaces #161's `EVEN`, and moves a centreline at most a line width, so that a Train, whose dot is three line widths across and is placed along its own track, stays on its stroke); and who may share a stretch (#165: Lines on the same level, with the gap narrowing past six Lines).
- #161's first slice still writes today's `Stroke`s, one for each Line on each stretch, so the map draws them unchanged. Their shape is the stretch's centreline, which the track carries as a shape of its own. `sides` stays each Line's side along its own shapes, so its Trains stay on their own track until they move onto their stretch's place. #176 moved them there: the bundle's `slots` give, all along each of a Line's shapes, the centreline its stroke is drawn along and its side, and how far along the centreline each distance along the shape is, within 25 m, so that below zoom 15 a Train sits on its stroke, along the centreline as its zoom band smooths it. Across a node it goes on the Line's curve: each curve names the lengths of centreline its ends take over, and a Train there goes as far along the curve, in proportion.
- A Line on a stretch is drawn off its own rails by its offset and by how far its track is from the centreline, at most NEAR, and its Trains can be that far from it. The Stations' dots stay on the rails. Zoomed right in, where APART goes to 0 and people follow a Train, the map draws each Line on its own track again, not its stretch's centreline. There, where Lines share a track, their strokes lie one over another, so that track is drawn in a neutral grey, and their names along it and their Trains tell them apart (#139). The map doesn't jump from one drawing to the other: over half a zoom (FADE) either side of where APART reaches 0, the stretches' strokes fade out over the rails as those fade in under them, while the Lines' names, the Trains and what a tap names still switch at once (#205).
- Lines of one Network and one colour, compared without case, take one place where they share a stretch, each stroke lying on the others', as Renfe's own map draws one C-4 up to its fork (#283): Madrid's C4, C4a and C4b, its C8a and C8b, FGC's RL1 and RL2, and Rodalies' R4 and RL4. Their Trains' pills keep their own Lines' names, and the line order and the narrowing past six Lines count places, which is the width drawn.
- Every change to how Lines are drawn reports #160's measures on the same track before and after.
- CONTEXT.md names an edge of the graph a **Stretch**, since "bundle" is the data bundle.
