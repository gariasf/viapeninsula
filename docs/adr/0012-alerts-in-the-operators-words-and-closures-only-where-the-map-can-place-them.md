# Alerts in the operators' own words, and Closures only where the map can place them

#279 has the map say what's gone wrong: part of a Line closed, buses in place of Trains, a single track. Its research note ([docs/research/alerts.md](../research/alerts.md), 7 Oct 2026) found that Renfe's alerts name Lines or a Station, never a stretch, with a start, no end and a text, Spanish but for Rodalies' second entry in Catalan; that TRAM's name Lines, with dates, the stretch in their text, in Catalan, Spanish and English; and that TMB's and TRAM's terms forbid altering what they publish. Decided with the maintainer in #279's grill, 7 Oct 2026:

- **Sources:** Renfe's alerts and TRAM's. TMB's, all about getting into a Station, and FGC's, notes on single Trips, can come later, each in a ticket of its own.
- **The operators' own words, never translated.** An Alert shows in the viewer's language where its feed has it, and otherwise in the feed's own, marked with its language and whose words they are. An English-speaking viewer reads Renfe's in Spanish.
- **All of them, as given.** Every Alert on a Line or a Station shows, newest first, with when it began. None is hidden for its age or its kind, and Live Trains don't overrule its words: they already show what runs beside an Alert gone stale. Where its Closure hides Trains, they lift that (#345, below).
- **The map draws only the Closures it can place:** where an Alert's words, read with their verb, or the timetable's replacement buses put a Line's Trains out of two of its Stations on its track, or down to a single track between them. Every other Alert is text: on a tap on a Closure or a stroke, in a followed Train's card, on a Station's board, and in #318's Now.
- **Alerts are a file of their own on R2,** `alerts.json`, which the fetcher writes when it changes, beside the snapshot (ADR-0003).

## Considered Options

- **Machine-translating Renfe's Alerts**, which its CC BY 4.0 licence allows. Rejected: TMB's and TRAM's terms forbid it for theirs, a closure translated wrong misleads, and the operator's words are what a viewer can check.
- **A mark on the map for every Alert**, at its Station or along its Line. Rejected: most Alerts name no place, and the map already carries Trains, names and badges.
- **Hiding Alerts older than some days, or letting Live Trains overrule a Line-wide one.** Rejected: guesses that could hide a real closure. A feed's file that has stood unchanged for days is another matter, below (#735).
- **A Closure hiding its Line's Trains for as long as its Alert is listed,** as #345 was first built. Rejected (the maintainer, 10 Oct 2026): Renfe's Alerts have no end, and on 10 Oct its 7 Oct Alerts closing R1 from Blanes to Maçanet-Massanes and R3 from Ripoll to Puigcerdà were still listed while both Lines ran there, so Tordera's board showed every R1 not stopping.
- **Closing a followed Train's panel as it leaves the map at a Closure, with a line saying why, or leaving it to close with nothing said.** Rejected (the maintainer, 10 Oct 2026, #420): a Trip that runs on is still a Trip, and its card is where its Line's Alerts are, so the panel stays, as a Trip still to come.
- **A Renfe Alert hiding Trains only on the service day it began.** Rejected: a closure that lasts days would stop hiding on its second.
- **Drawing a TRAM Alert's Closure for its whole period**, as #341 first did, or reading the dates from its words ("Els dies 10, 11 i 12 d'octubre", "de 7:30 a 14:30 h"), or both. Rejected (the maintainer, 10 Oct 2026, #397): the first drew T1–T3 closed from 1 to 9 Oct while trams ran across, and the words are free Catalan, Spanish and English, where a miss would draw a Closure where trams run, which is the fault the timetable's rule fixes.
- **A TRAM Alert's Closure in force by each of its Lines' own Trips.** Rejected (the maintainer, 11 Oct 2026, #397): T1 has 5, 1 and 2 Trips on 10–12 Oct, so its stroke would have been hatched only within half an hour of them, three times on the 10th and by day not at all on the 11th and 12th, beside T2's and T3's, which are hatched all day.
- **Alerts in the snapshot.** Rejected: they rarely change, and the page keeps every snapshot for 35 minutes.
- **A pill under the legend counting the Alerts in view.** Rejected: the banner pill is for live data's state, and the list is Now's.

## Consequences

- ADR-0003 is amended when the fetcher writes `alerts.json`.
- The timetable's Closures come in each day's bundle, beside its Trips, from the daily build, and its report lists where a Line's replacement buses run where the Line has no track (#340). A Closure is only of a part of the buses' run that none of the Line's Trains runs that day, as Renfe runs most of its replacement buses beside the Line's Trains, not in their place: on 7 Oct, closing each bus's whole run on the Line's track made 14 Closures, and 11 had the Line's Trains there in the same hours (the maintainer, 9 Oct 2026).
- An Alert whose words are about some of a Line's Trains makes no Closure of its Line, though its words close a stretch: it's where those Trains don't run, not the Line's. It is one named by where it leaves, "Tren con salida a las 09:27h de la estación de BENACAZÓN…", or those to or from a place, "para los trenes con origen/destino Madrid", the two wordings 9 Oct's file has. On 10 Oct that was Sevilla's two C5 Alerts of 7 Oct, one Train each, and its C3's plan for Madrid's Trains between Los Rosales and Cazalla-Constantina, where C3's own six Trains a day ran (two of them Live there that day). It stays text, as does an Alert that also closes its Line in such words (#396).
- A TRAM Alert's closed Closure is in force only at the moments its timetable runs the Trains of the Alert's Lines short of it, where Renfe's is in force while its Alert is listed (the maintainer, 10 Oct 2026, #397). Renfe's Alerts have no end, and a Live Train within lifts what its Closure hides (#345, below). TRAM's have a period, which is when the Alert shows, not when its works are: `sc-280` showed from 1 Oct for works on 10–12 Oct, while trams ran across Francesc Macià – Montesa. So it is in force at a moment where some Trip of any of the Alert's Lines runs within half an hour either side and none of those calls at both its Stations, as the day's file has T1–T3 turn back at Montesa on 10–12 Oct. The Alert's Lines' Trips go together, T1's with T2's and T3's, as the Alert is one (the maintainer, 11 Oct 2026). Where none runs, as at night, or one crosses it, it isn't: not drawn, and hiding nothing. The Alert's period still bounds it from outside, its words are never read for dates, and a single track, which a Line's Trains run through, keeps its Alert's period.
- Where no Train of a Line calls at the closed part's Stations in the whole timetable, as on R3 south of La Garriga on 7 Oct, the map has no track to draw its Closure on, and the Alert stays text.
- Within a closed Closure, between its two Stations, a Train isn't drawn, and a Station's board shows it not stopping at those between them, nor leaving either of them into it, as if its Trip ended there, unless it's Live within it, as live data wins (#345). The Stations between a Closure's two are those its Line's Trips that call at both call at between them, and a Train that runs past them, as R2 Sud's faster Trains run past Garraf, is within it where it's between the two along its track, as those Trips have them. A single track hides no Train. On 7 Oct, from 13:00 to 13:45, that left off the map two R1 Trains drawn between Blanes and Maçanet-Massanes for 18.8 Train-minutes, neither of them Cancelled then (#345's count). A followed Train that runs into a closed Closure stays in its panel, as a Trip still to come that doesn't run between the Closure's Stations, with its Line's Alerts, and its strip marks the Stations within as not stopped at, until it comes out at the far side or its Trip ends (#420).
- Once a report has a Live Train of its Line within a closed Closure an Alert makes, the Closure hides none of that Line's Trains, as if it weren't there, until the Alert changes, its period, effect or words, or a new Alert closes that part: live data lifts the hiding, where it doesn't overrule the words (the maintainer, 10 Oct 2026). The report has to pin the Train to a Station between the Closure's two, or put it more than 200 m within them along its track, by its GPS, no further than 200 m from that track, or TRAM's distance, and not be of a Cancelled Train, so that one standing at either Station, or that Renfe ran no further, lifts nothing. A report by Train number, as Renfe's long-distance feed's, counts on the Trip it's matched to, as a Train drawn by it is, and only where its GPS is that close to that Trip's track, not on another part of the Train's run; a report of the Trip from its Network's own feed goes first (#428). The Closure is still drawn hatched, with its Alert's words, and a tap on it adds a line under them while it's lifted: "R1's Trains have run here since 12:13", the time one was first seen within it, which goes with the lift (#422, the maintainer, 10 Oct 2026: no new look). Each Line's is its own, as T1's, T2's and T3's from Francesc Macià to Montesa are. The page remembers what it has lifted for as long as it's open, so one opened while a stale Alert's Line runs hides its Trains until one is seen there again. On 7 Oct, from 13:00 to 13:45, that lifted R3's and T1–T3's from the first snapshot, as their Trains ran there (TRAM's Closures then showed for the whole of their Alerts' periods, which #397 ended), and not R1's: no report had a running R1 Train within Blanes – Maçanet-Massanes, only the Cancelled one Renfe pinned to Tordera.
- Where a feed's file last changed over 48 h before now, as `alerts.json` has it, its Alerts' Closures hide no Trains, as a lifted one doesn't: still drawn hatched, with their Alerts' words, and those Alerts still listed, with the file's age (#390) (the maintainer, 12 Oct 2026, #735). Renfe's stood unchanged from 7 Oct 07:32 UTC, so its 7 Oct Closures of R1 from Blanes to Maçanet-Massanes, R3 from Ripoll to Puigcerdà and San Sebastián's C1 from Brinkola to Zumarraga hid those Lines' Scheduled Trains at every page open, until a Live Train was seen within, for days after their Trains ran again. It goes by the whole file, not each Alert, as Renfe's Alerts have no end and a file that has stood says nothing of which are still true; so C1's Irun – Hernani, of 14 Aug, stops hiding with them, its works still in the timetable's own Closures (#340). TRAM's file changes at each read, so the rule meets Renfe's alone. Renfe publishes no other alerts file: data.renfe.com's `incidencias-avisos` lists the same `alerts.pb` and `alerts.json`, both last modified 7 Oct 07:32:52 GMT on 12 Oct.
- A Station's board names the Lines whose timetable calls at its Station on any day, whether or not a Trip of them calls there on the days on the map, as where works stop one, and with or without an Alert on it (#529, the maintainer, 11 Oct 2026): the track names each Station's Lines, as it names its Networks (#392), and the board shows those Lines' Alerts, with their pills, though it lists no departures for a Line no Trip of calls. It names them before the Trips have come too (#594), so that its pills don't change as they do. #392 named a Line no Trip of calls only where an Alert was on it. No Alert is read for a Station from its words (#396's reader) or from its Network's other Lines.
- Long distance has no alerts feed (Renfe's `alerts_LD` answers 404), so its road sections inside a run (#269) wait for long distance (#258).
